# AI-SPEC — Phase 21: Authoritative Personalized Inventory

> AI design contract generated by `$gsd-ai-integration-phase`. Consumed by `gsd-planner` and `gsd-eval-auditor`.
> This phase is explicitly classified as deterministic extraction: no AI/LLM framework or model call belongs in its runtime path.

---

## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** Extraction

**Description:**
Router discovers locally installed Claude and Codex capabilities, normalizes adapter evidence into a framework-neutral schema, and reconciles incremental filesystem changes with authoritative scans. Good output is deterministic, current, provenance-preserving, safely scoped, and suitable for later routing decisions without any model or network dependency.

**Critical Failure Modes:**
1. Unsafe or out-of-scope filesystem content becomes trusted inventory or executable policy.
2. Incremental and authoritative reconciliation produce different canonical results.
3. Capability identity, provenance, lifecycle state, or dependency relationships become stale or incorrect.

## 1b. Domain Context

> Researched by `gsd-domain-researcher`. Grounds the evaluation strategy in domain expert knowledge.

**Industry Vertical:** Local developer tooling, configuration management, and software-component inventory

**User Population:** Developers and Router maintainers operating heterogeneous user, project, and worktree-scoped Claude and Codex installations.

**Stakes Level:** High

**Output Consequence:** The canonical inventory becomes the authority for later contract inference, gap analysis, routing eligibility, invalidation, and lifecycle decisions. A false positive can make untrusted or unavailable local content appear dispatchable; a false negative or stale record can preserve broken routes or hide an installed capability.

### What Domain Experts Evaluate Against

**Dimension: Installed-state fidelity**  
**Good (domain expert would accept):** Every in-scope installed artifact is represented once at the required granularity, including non-dispatchable configuration, instruction, container, disabled, and unknown records; runtime, scope, enabled state, lifecycle role, and dependency availability match an independently enumerated fixture filesystem.  
**Bad (domain expert would flag):** A supported artifact is omitted, an unknown type disappears, two simultaneously live copies are merged, one installation is counted twice, or a non-dispatchable record receives a fabricated invocation.  
**Stakes:** Critical  
**Source:** NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5.1 CM-8 requires an inventory that accurately reflects the system, includes all components, avoids duplicate accounting, and is updated during installation/removal; DISC-01, DISC-02, D-01 through D-04.

**Dimension: Authoritative convergence after change**  
**Good (domain expert would accept):** For add, edit, rename, move, disable, replace, dependency-loss, and removal sequences—including missed, duplicated, coalesced, and reordered watcher events—the incremental path and a clean authoritative rescan produce byte-identical semantic snapshots and the same transitive invalidations.  
**Bad (domain expert would flag):** Results depend on event order; a dropped event leaves stale state; volatile timestamps or generation IDs break equality; or a partial scan replaces the last complete authority.  
**Stakes:** Critical  
**Source:** Node.js documents that `fs.watch()` is platform-dependent, can omit filenames, is unreliable on some filesystems, and provides no protection against malicious filesystem actions; DISC-03 through DISC-05, D-05 through D-08, D-19 and D-20.

**Dimension: Identity and scope correctness**  
**Good (domain expert would accept):** Global, user, project, and worktree installations remain distinct; rename/move continuity is accepted only from a validated stable ID or an unambiguous one-removed/one-added exact fingerprint match; replacements receive new identity unless that proof exists.  
**Bad (domain expert would flag):** Same-named records across scopes are merged, equal content at two live paths is collapsed, similarity transfers trust, or a replacement inherits stale corrections and relationships.  
**Stakes:** Critical  
**Source:** NIST CM-8 requires component accountability and system association; DISC-02, DISC-05, D-09 through D-13 and D-15.

**Dimension: Filesystem authorization boundary**  
**Good (domain expert would accept):** Discovery begins only from caller-authorized logical roots, resolves and revalidates real paths before reading, rejects traversal, escaping or cyclic symlinks with diagnostics, and treats artifact-authored text as inert evidence rather than policy or executable configuration.  
**Bad (domain expert would flag):** A path string is trusted before canonicalization, an escaping symlink is followed, a file grants itself scope or dispatchability, or inspection of one root reads unrelated home/project content.  
**Stakes:** Critical  
**Source:** OWASP path-traversal and symlink-attack guidance; Node.js `fs.watch()` security caveats; DISC-08 and D-14 through D-16.

**Dimension: Inspectable provenance without disclosure**  
**Good (domain expert would accept):** Operators can trace each record to runtime, logical root, relative path, scope, native/semantic type, fingerprint, adapter/parser version, dependency state, lifecycle state, and diagnostics, while raw instruction/configuration bodies, secret values, and developer-specific absolute paths are omitted or redacted by default.  
**Bad (domain expert would flag):** A record cannot be traced to its source and parser, provenance becomes stale after mutation, inspection emits tokens or raw authored content, or capability metadata leaves the local machine.  
**Stakes:** High  
**Source:** NIST SSDF PS.3.2 calls for collecting and maintaining component provenance; GDPR Article 5 data-minimisation, accuracy, storage-limitation, and integrity/confidentiality principles where inventory metadata is personal data; D-17, HLTH-01 and HLTH-02.

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

- **Watcher truth drift:** coalesced, reordered, missing-filename, root-replacement, or dropped events leave the incremental view different from a clean scan. The watcher is a dirty-root hint; it is not the authority.
- **Partial-scan authority loss:** an unreadable root, transient read error, link cycle, or permission change is interpreted as removal and replaces the last complete snapshot, invalidating valid capabilities.
- **Identity contamination:** same-named cross-scope records or identical live copies are merged, or a merely similar replacement inherits the removed capability's trust, corrections, and routes.
- **Unsafe discovery or inspection:** symlink/path escape reads outside an authorized root, capability-authored prose promotes itself to policy/invocation, or inspection leaks secrets, raw instruction bodies, usernames, and absolute local paths.

### Regulatory / Compliance Context

No sector-specific regulation is identified for this local developer-tool deployment. The binding product constraints are stricter than a typical cloud inventory: discovery and analysis remain local, operate only within explicitly authorized roots, collect only the allowlisted metadata needed for reconciliation, redact secret values and raw bodies, and never treat discovery as authorization.

For deployments processing EU personal data, filesystem paths, usernames, repository names, and authored metadata can identify a developer and therefore may fall within GDPR. Article 5 directly supports purpose limitation, data minimisation, accuracy/currentness, storage limitation, integrity/confidentiality, and demonstrable accountability. This section does not assert that every local installation creates a separate regulated processing activity; deployment ownership and data flows determine applicability.

NIST CM-8 and SSDF PS.3.2 are evaluation baselines rather than laws for this deployment: they support complete/current component accountability and maintained provenance. Filesystem safety is enforced through canonical containment, least-scope reads, symlink rejection/revalidation, bounded traversal, and last-known-good retention—not through trusting watcher events or capability content.

### Specific Pitfalls

1. **Treating watcher events as truth.** `fs.watch()` events can be coalesced,
   reordered, lack filenames, or be dropped. Events select dirty roots only;
   canonical rescans establish truth.
2. **Promoting an incomplete scan.** Read, access, cycle, or root-replacement
   uncertainty must publish `degraded`/stale diagnostics without replacing the
   last complete fingerprint or semantic snapshot.
3. **Fabricating invocation for inert records.** Configuration, instruction,
   container, disabled, and unknown records remain inspectable but
   non-dispatchable; invocation is required only for schema-validated
   dispatchable records.
4. **Equating identical content with one identity.** Two simultaneously live
   paths remain distinct. Exact fingerprints prove rename/move continuity only
   for an unambiguous one-removed/one-added pairing.
5. **Mixing operational metadata into semantic bytes.** Timestamps, event
   order, trigger names, scan IDs, and generation IDs belong in inspection
   state, not the byte-identical convergence snapshot.

## 4. Implementation Guidance

**Model and parameters:** None. Runtime model provider, model name,
temperature, token limits, embeddings, and network endpoints are prohibited in
this phase. Extraction parameters are deterministic resource limits instead:
bounded authorized roots, maximum artifact size/nesting, sorted traversal,
single-flight reconciliation, bounded debounce/max latency, and a bounded
periodic authoritative repair interval.

### Core Pattern

```js
import { acquireRegistry, assembleRegistry } from './build.mjs';
import { diffFingerprintTrees } from './diff.mjs';
import { scanFingerprintTree } from './fingerprint.mjs';
import { reconcileCandidate } from './reconcile.mjs';
import { stableStringify } from './schema.mjs';

export async function reconcileAuthoritative({
  roots,
  previousFingerprint,
  previousSemanticBytes,
  acquisitionOptions,
}) {
  const currentFingerprint = await scanFingerprintTree(roots);

  // Completeness must be derived from explicit root diagnostics. Keep this
  // local predicate conservative until the schema owns a named helper.
  const blockingCodes = new Set([
    'access_denied', 'read_error', 'scan_error', 'path_escape',
  ]);
  const incomplete = currentFingerprint.diagnostics
    .filter(item => blockingCodes.has(item.code));
  if (incomplete.length > 0) {
    return {
      promoted: false,
      state: 'degraded',
      diagnostics: incomplete,
      semanticBytes: previousSemanticBytes,
      fingerprint: previousFingerprint,
    };
  }

  const lifecycle = diffFingerprintTrees(
    previousFingerprint,
    currentFingerprint,
  );
  const acquisition = acquireRegistry(acquisitionOptions);
  const { registry, diagnostics = [] } = assembleRegistry(acquisition);

  // Reconciliation validates the whole candidate before any downstream mapper
  // or activation callback is allowed to observe it.
  const verdict = reconcileCandidate({
    candidate: registry,
    lifecycle,
  });
  const semanticBytes = stableStringify(registry);
  const promoted = verdict.disposition === 'eligible';

  return {
    promoted,
    state: promoted ? 'current' : 'failed',
    diagnostics,
    fingerprint: currentFingerprint,
    semanticBytes,
    verdict,
  };
}
```

The snippet demonstrates transaction order, not a replacement watcher. Exact
option and result shapes must follow the existing module contracts when the
planner evolves them; newly introduced completeness and operational-state
helpers must be unit-tested before use.

### Parsing and Tool Configuration

- Declare filesystem roots through trusted caller configuration. Canonicalize
  each root and target with `realpath`, then verify target containment using a
  path-separator-aware comparison before reading.
- Keep native layout and parser versions in adapters. Router core receives
  normalized semantic types plus namespaced native evidence and never branches
  on Claude, Codex, GSD, or plugin names.
- Treat frontmatter, prose, JSON values, and plugin metadata as untrusted data.
  They may contribute fingerprints and allowlisted evidence, but cannot grant
  scope, permissions, dispatchability, policy, or executable invocation.
- Retain unknown authorized artifacts as `semantic_type: "unknown"` with native
  type, provenance, scope, fingerprint, and adapter evidence; force
  `dispatchable: false`.
- Represent compound installations as a container record plus member records
  connected by provenance. A container is not dispatchable merely because a
  member is.

### State Management

Maintain two explicit state planes:

1. **Semantic authority:** sorted records, evidence, enabled/dependency state,
   and relationship references. This value is canonicalized and compared
   byte-for-byte across incremental and authoritative construction.
2. **Operational inspection:** active/candidate generation IDs, trigger,
   timestamps, pending changes, stale/unreadable roots, and
   `current | reconciling | degraded | failed`. This state is observable but
   excluded from semantic bytes.

Only a complete, schema-valid, fully invalidated, mapped, verified candidate may
replace active authority. Any scan, parse, dependency, reconciliation, mapping,
or publication failure retains the last-known-good active generation.

### Reconciliation and Identity

- Both `refreshIncrementalAcquisition()` and `acquireRegistry()` must terminate
  at the same `assembleRegistry()` contract.
- Apply validated declared stable-ID continuity first. Otherwise pair a removed
  and added record only when each is the sole member of its exact-fingerprint
  bucket. Similarity remains advisory.
- Seed invalidation from removal, replacement, disablement, and unavailable
  required dependencies. Traverse reverse reference edges to a fixed point over
  aliases, equivalence/workflow references, corrections, mappings, and compiled
  routes before mapping begins.
- Sort records, evidence, graph edges, diagnostics, and verdicts at their
  schema-owned boundaries. Never rely on discovery, map, set, or event order.

### Context Strategy

There is no model context window. The analogous resource boundary is bounded
filesystem and output context: reject oversized/deep artifacts, exclude known
cache/dependency/VCS trees, hash rather than expose raw bodies, bound inspection
arrays with total/returned/truncated metadata, and stream no unvalidated partial
authority to consumers.

## 4b. AI Systems Best Practices

> For this non-AI phase, translate these concerns into explicit deterministic-runtime constraints.

### 4b.1 Structured Outputs Without Pydantic

Pydantic is not applicable to the Node.js runtime, and no LLM output exists to
validate or retry. The equivalent contract is the existing JavaScript schema:
every adapter observation is normalized, passed through
`validateCapability()`, canonicalized with `canonicalizeCapability()`, and
serialized with `stableStringify()` before publication.

Schema failure is deterministic and must not be retried as though it were a
sampling error. Log the diagnostic code, adapter/parser version, logical root,
relative path, and redacted field path; never log raw authored bodies or secret
values. A transient filesystem read may be retried only by the bounded watcher
repair policy. Persistent schema/parse failure surfaces as degraded or failed
state while last-known-good authority remains active.

### 4b.2 Async-First Control Plane

Use `async`/`await` for scans, watcher orchestration, and atomic publication so
the background control plane does not block prompt-time routing. Maintain one
in-flight reconciliation with a rerun flag; do not launch overlapping scans
that can race baseline advancement.

The common Node mistake is mixing unbounded synchronous tree I/O into the
long-running watcher or calling async work without awaiting it. Synchronous
helpers may remain in bounded CLI/startup or existing adapter seams, but bulk
authoritative traversal should use promise APIs and explicit concurrency
limits. Streaming partial records is inappropriate: await complete validation
and reconciliation, then atomically publish one candidate.

### 4b.3 Input-Contract Discipline

There are no system prompts, user prompts, few-shot examples, or `max_tokens`.
Preserve the same separation at the data boundary:

- trusted caller configuration defines authorized roots, scope, and policy;
- untrusted artifact content supplies data/evidence only;
- adapter code interprets native formats through versioned allowlists;
- schema and reconciliation code alone determine dispatchability and safety.

Fixture examples belong in tests, not as runtime heuristics. Do not infer types,
permissions, identity, or invocation from prose similarity.

### 4b.4 Bounded Filesystem Context

There is no context window to compact, summarize, or rerank. Bound the
deterministic input set by declared roots, containment, exclusions, file-size
and nesting limits, and canonical sorting. Preserve unknown types as compact
metadata plus fingerprints rather than raw bodies. When inspection output is
large, paginate or truncate the redacted projection without truncating the
authoritative internal snapshot.

Incremental state is an optimization, not memory. Startup, scheduled repair,
ambiguous/dropped events, watcher restart, root replacement, and fingerprint
mismatch trigger a clean authoritative scan. Incomplete scans retain the last
complete snapshot instead of summarizing or guessing missing content.

### 4b.5 Cost and Latency Budget

Model cost is exactly **$0 per call** and expected model-call volume is
**zero**. Any provider request, embedding generation, semantic cache, or
model-based classification in this phase is an architecture regression.

Budget local work instead:

- hash and compare root/subtree fingerprints to skip unchanged parsing;
- debounce event bursts while enforcing bounded maximum latency;
- keep reconciliation single-flight and coalesce reruns;
- cache only exact, fingerprint-addressed deterministic results;
- schedule bounded authoritative repair scans;
- measure scan duration, records/files examined, bytes read, cache reuse,
  reconciliation duration, stale-root count, and time since last complete scan.

No semantic cache is permitted: approximate matches cannot establish identity,
type, trust, or current inventory state.

## 5. Evaluation Strategy

Phase 21 is deterministic extraction with no model, prompt, embedding, or
network runtime. Evaluation is therefore entirely code-based: exact fixture
oracles, schema validation, canonical byte comparison, callback-order
assertions, and filesystem abuse cases. LLM judges and model-eval platforms
would weaken reproducibility and are not permitted in the runtime or CI path.

### Evaluation Rubrics

| Dimension | Priority | PASS | FAIL | Measurement |
|-----------|----------|------|------|-------------|
| Installed-state and schema fidelity | Critical | Every artifact independently enumerated inside an authorized synthetic root appears exactly once at the required container/member granularity with correct runtime, scope, native/semantic type, lifecycle role, enabled/dispatchable state, dependencies, and provenance; unknowns remain visible and non-dispatchable. | Any supported or unknown artifact disappears, one installation is counted twice, two live copies are merged, a non-dispatchable artifact gains fabricated invocation, or a required field is invalid. | Code — fixture oracle plus `validateCapability()`, canonicalization, uniqueness, and field-invariant assertions. |
| Authoritative convergence and task completion | Critical | After every add, edit, rename, move, disable, replace, dependency-loss, and removal sequence, all missed/duplicate/coalesced/reordered/filename-less event permutations produce the exact same `stableStringify()` semantic bytes and invalidation result as a clean scan. | Event order changes semantic bytes, a dropped event preserves stale state, operational metadata enters the comparison, or any supported mutation fails to converge. | Code — exhaustive table/property matrix in `router.inventory-convergence.test.mjs`; zero mismatches allowed. |
| Identity and scope continuity | Critical | Global, user, project, and worktree copies remain distinct; continuity occurs only through a validated stable ID or an unambiguous one-removed/one-added exact-fingerprint match; replacement and simultaneous duplicates retain separate identities. | Same-name cross-scope records merge, similarity transfers identity/trust, an ambiguous fingerprint pair is joined, or a replacement inherits stale corrections or references. | Code — identity fixtures and exact expected before/after ID sets. |
| Transitive invalidation | Critical | Removal, replacement, disablement, or required-dependency loss invalidates every reachable alias, equivalence/workflow reference, correction, mapping, and compiled route in the same candidate transaction before mapper or activation callbacks run. | Any stale downstream reference survives, invalidation depends on graph iteration order, or a callback observes the uninvalidation candidate. | Code — multi-hop graph closure oracle, permutation tests, and callback-order assertions. |
| Filesystem authorization and untrusted-content safety | Critical | Every read starts from a caller-authorized logical root, canonical real paths remain contained, safe in-root links are revalidated, escaping/cyclic links are excluded with diagnostics, and authored prose cannot grant policy, scope, invocation, or dispatchability. | Traversal or symlink escape is read, a cycle is followed, string-prefix containment is trusted, or artifact content promotes itself to executable authority. | Code — adversarial temporary-root fixtures; zero outside-root reads and zero unsafe promotions allowed. |
| Provenance and redaction | High | Inspection provides the allowlisted logical root, relative path, runtime, scope, types, state, fingerprint, adapter/parser version, dependencies, lifecycle, provenance, and diagnostics while excluding raw bodies, secret values, and developer-specific absolute paths. | A record cannot be traced to its source/parser, provenance stays stale after mutation, output includes fixture secrets/raw text/absolute roots, or arrays omit truncation metadata. | Code — exact output-shape allowlist plus seeded canary-secret and absolute-path non-occurrence assertions. |
| Last-known-good retention | Critical | Incomplete, unreadable, unsafe, parse-invalid, reconciliation-invalid, mapping-failed, or publication-failed candidates expose `degraded`/`failed` operational state while preserving the exact active semantic bytes and last complete fingerprint baseline. | A partial scan becomes authoritative, uncertain absence becomes removal, the baseline advances, or active bytes change after any failed stage. | Code — fault injection at every stage and byte equality against the pre-failure active snapshot. |

Safety is represented by filesystem authorization, inert authored content,
redaction, and fail-closed publication. Task completion is represented by exact
incremental-to-authoritative convergence across every required profile and
mutation, not by a subjective score.

schema, convergence, safety, redaction, invalidation, or retention failures;
flaky retries do not convert a failure into a pass.

## 6. Guardrails

Guardrails are deliberately limited to failures that could corrupt authority,
escape filesystem authorization, disclose local data, or expose stale routes.
They run in the background control plane and must never add work to prompt-time
routing.

### Online Guardrails

| Critical failure mode | Every-request check | Blocking action |
|-----------------------|---------------------|-----------------|
| Path/symlink escape or untrusted self-authorization | Canonicalize root and target, verify separator-aware containment and cycle state before each read; validate dispatch-relevant fields against adapter/schema authority. | Do not read or normalize the target; emit a redacted diagnostic and keep it out of the candidate. |
| Invalid or incomplete candidate | Require complete-root status, schema validity, canonicalization, and successful reconciliation before baseline advancement. | Mark operational state `degraded` or `failed`; retain last-complete fingerprint and semantic snapshot. |
| Stale transitive references | Compute invalidation closure and verify no live reference targets a removed, replaced, disabled, or dependency-unhealthy record before mapper invocation. | Abort mapping/publication and preserve active authority. |
| Inspection disclosure | Project through a field allowlist, relative/logical paths, secret/body suppression, and bounded result metadata. | Refuse unsafe fields, redact diagnostic detail, and return a stable reason code. |
| Publication race or verification failure | Enforce single-flight reconciliation, immutable candidate bytes, guarded verification, and version-consistent atomic publication. | Reject candidate activation; retain the exact last-known-good generation and schedule bounded repair. |

### Offline Flywheel

| Signal | Batch evaluation | Improvement action |
|--------|------------------|--------------------|
| Convergence mismatch | Replay the exact root fixture, mutation stream, watcher anomaly, incremental path, and clean scan; compare canonical bytes and invalidation sets. | Add the minimized case to the permanent corpus before fixing canonicalization or scheduling. |
| Unknown or parse-diagnostic growth | Group local redacted diagnostics by adapter, native type, parser version, logical root, and reason code. | Add or revise adapter handling while preserving opaque non-dispatchable fallback. |
| Degraded/stale-root recurrence | Replay sampled root-replacement, permission, cycle, and partial-read conditions with the fake clock. | Refine completeness classification or repair scheduling; never relax promotion gates. |
| Identity ambiguity | Review ambiguous exact-fingerprint buckets and replacement evidence without auto-pairing. | Add fixtures or adapter stable-ID support; similarity remains advisory. |
| Redaction regression | Scan every fixture inspection output for seeded secrets, raw bodies, absolute temp roots, and usernames. | Block release and extend the allowlist/non-occurrence corpus. |

All flywheel inputs remain local and bounded. They contain reason codes,
portable provenance, fingerprints, counts, and timings—not raw authored bodies
or arbitrary filesystem content.

## 7. Production Monitoring

Use the existing local structured diagnostics and `router-control` inspection
surface as the tracing system. Each reconciliation records a bounded local
transaction summary: trigger, active/candidate generation IDs, affected
logical roots, start/end/duration, completeness, stage disposition, semantic
fingerprint, pending-change count, diagnostic reason codes, and whether the
last-known-good snapshot was retained. Do not record raw file bodies, secret
values, developer-specific absolute paths, prompt data, or remote telemetry.

| Metric | Healthy threshold | Alert / operator state |
|--------|-------------------|------------------------|
| Model/provider/network calls | Exactly `0` | Any call is an architecture and privacy regression; fail CI/release. |
| Candidate promotion safety | `100%` of promotions complete, schema-valid, fully invalidated, mapped, verified, and atomically published | Any gate bypass is critical; reject promotion and mark `failed`. |
| Incremental/full convergence | `100%` byte equality in scheduled sampling and verification corpus | Any mismatch marks `degraded`, retains active authority, and triggers immediate authoritative repair. |
| Outside-root reads / inspection secret leaks | Exactly `0` | Critical security alert; reject candidate/output and require release-blocking investigation. |
| Stale live references after invalidation | Exactly `0` | Critical; abort publication and report invalidated target/reference reason codes. |
| Last-known-good mutation on failed/partial scan | Exactly `0` bytes changed | Critical; block activation and recovery advancement. |
| Scan freshness | `current` after startup and within the configured repair interval after the last complete scan | At one missed interval show `degraded` with stale roots/reason; at two intervals raise an operator alert while retaining authority. |
| Reconciliation scheduling | One in flight; reruns coalesced; dirty-root latency within configured `maxLatencyMs` | Overlap, queue growth, or breached max latency raises degraded state and a scheduling diagnostic. |
| Diagnostic and unknown-type rate | Baseline per adapter/parser version and profile | Alert on a new build-blocking reason code or a 2× increase over the rolling local baseline with at least 5 observations. |
| Resource bounds | Artifact size/depth, bytes read, files examined, result limits, and scan duration remain within configured bounds | Reject oversized/deep input; mark truncated inspection explicitly; alert when p95 scan/reconcile duration exceeds 2× its established local baseline over 20 complete scans. |

### Sampling Strategy

- Run online containment, schema, completeness, invalidation, redaction, and
  publication checks on **every** scan or inspection request.
- Compare incremental semantic bytes with a clean authoritative scan at
  startup, on the bounded repair schedule, after ambiguous/dropped events,
  watcher restart, root replacement, fingerprint mismatch, and before release.
- Retain bounded summaries for the latest **20 complete reconciliations** and
  all current degraded/failed roots; compact older healthy summaries locally.
- Weight offline review toward new adapter/parser versions, unknown types,
  identity ambiguities, incomplete scans, repeated repairs, and any
  convergence mismatch. Healthy unchanged scans need only aggregate counts and
  latency.
- Add every confirmed production defect as a minimized checked-in reference
  scenario, then require it in the full CI gate.
